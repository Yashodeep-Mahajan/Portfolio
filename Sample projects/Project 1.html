How to Secure Your Wi-Fi Network

Why It Matters

Securing your Wi-Fi network protects your personal data, prevents unauthorized access, and ensures your internet bandwidth is not misused.

Step-by-Step Guide

  1. Change the Default Router Login Credentials:
    Access your router’s admin panel (usually 192.168.1.1) and change the default username and password to something strong and unique.
  2. Set a Strong Wi-Fi Password:
    Use a complex password with a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols. Avoid using names or common words.
  3. Use WPA3 or WPA2 Encryption:
    Go to your router settings and ensure your wireless security is set to WPA2 or WPA3 (if available). Avoid WEP, which is outdated and insecure.
  4. Disable WPS (Wi-Fi Protected Setup):
    WPS is known to have vulnerabilities. Turn it off to improve security.
  5. Hide Your Network SSID (Optional):
    You can hide your network name from being broadcasted. However, this is not foolproof, and may cause inconvenience with new device connections.
  6. Keep Your Router Firmware Updated:
    Regularly check the router manufacturer’s website for firmware updates and apply them to patch security flaws.
  7. Limit Connected Devices:
    Monitor and restrict which devices are allowed to connect to your network using MAC address filtering.
  8. Turn Off Remote Access:
    Disable remote access to your router’s admin panel unless it’s absolutely necessary.
